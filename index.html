Is this you?

Any of these sound familiar?

  • You want more harmony at home. Not that anything is wrong exactly, just less chaos, less angst, things feeling aligned.
  • You split the household work fairly, everyone agreed, and it still leaks into the gaps nobody named.
  • You want to be visible enough in your work that you can say no to what doesn't light you up. Right now you're saying yes to things you can't afford to refuse.
  • There's an ask you've been sitting on, for something your household needs, because it might inconvenience somebody.
  • You're unsatisfied with your home environment in more ways than you say out loud.
  • You want your family healthy. Long enough to run with your grandchildren.
  • Your kids are getting older and you can count the years left. You want time and resources for them, and you want to build something for their future.
  • You already know what to do. You've read the books, taken the course, listened to the podcast. You're still not doing it.

Every one of those came from a real household in the last week. Not one of them is a knowledge problem.

The question

What do I want for my household?

Four things.

  • PersonTake care of myself. Physically, mentally, emotionally, socially.
  • PartnerBe a loving and supportive partner to my wife.
  • ParentBe a present and nurturing dad to our daughter.
  • ProviderProvide for my family, without compromising the first three.

I believe a lot of other people want these too. Here's my rub:

  • I sometimes eat garbage and don't take care of my body.
  • I sometimes feel disconnected from my wife, and scared to have the hard conversations.
  • I don't give my daughter my full attention, and I get frustrated with her.
  • I don't take bold, courageous action to provide for my family.

It isn't for lack of wanting. It's inaction instead of action. Consuming instead of producing. Hiding instead of putting myself out there. Underneath all of it: fear of rejection, fear of getting it right, fear of unworthiness. Which are all kind of the same fear.

And here's the thing. The voice in my head tells me that list far more often than it tells me this one:

  • PersonMy rageful temper no longer consumes me or gets aimed at people in my household. My fear and helplessness get expressed in the moment, instead of carried for a year.
  • PartnerWe found the house of our dreams, survived a four-year fertility struggle, and are raising a daughter who carries both our distinct gifts.
  • ParentYesterday was a Monday off school. We made slime, ate breakfast outside, walked through town, went to the library, got water ice. I'm the dad I said I'd be more often than not.
  • ProviderI've worked for billion-dollar global financial institutions, in underserved inner-city communities, built new models for social impact, and coached people who affect the world and are trying to be more themselves while doing it.

Both lists are true on the same day. Getting good at holding both, out loud, with other people, is most of the work.

The one idea

It's not a knowledge problem

We have every bit of knowledge that ever existed at our fingertips. It isn't that we don't know what to do. It's that an uncomfortable feeling gets there first. An uncertain one. And we want to feel those feelings and still act, empowered and imperfectly, right inside them.

So much of what's sold to us points outward: the answer is over there, and here's the clear pathway to the thing that will make you whole. This is the opposite. Feel the thing. Say it out loud. Run an experiment. Report back.

Yes, this is another screen. I don't want to do this on social media, and I believe real connection can happen virtually, in the right container, with the right agreements. The difference: not a screen you use to avoid a feeling, but one you come to in order to welcome it.

My hope is that what we practice here shows up in how we are at home, and in the physical communities we're already part of.

Who this is for

A household is whoever you share a home and a life with. One person, a couple, roommates, a family of any shape.

Households where at least one of the four is straining: person, partner, parent, provider.

Especially family businesses, solopreneurs and the self-employed, where work and home share a roof and a bank account.

People willing to own their part. We're all a victim of something, and the work is the boundaries and actions that empower us inside it.

Who this is not for

If you want a curriculum, or the five steps, I'd rather you kept your money.

If you're in crisis right now, you need more than a community. Please get real support first and come find us after. In the US, call or text 988.

And if talking honestly about your household makes you want to close this page, that's worth listening to. This asks something of you.

The rules of the room

How we are with each other

  • No advice. Share what happened to you. Let people take what's useful.
  • Bragging required. If you only bring the hard thing, we'll ask for the good one.
  • We grow what's good. Not only what needs fixing.
  • No gossip, blaming or shaming.
  • No ranking one household against another. A new angle, not a scoreboard.
  • We listen intently.
  • We hold each other accountable, even when it's uncomfortable. Saying the hard thing here is practice for saying it face to face.
  • What's shared here stays here.
  • No ads, no trolls, no performance.

What I can and cannot promise

I can't promise I'll get it right. Trying to get it right is most of what got me here.

I can promise caring spaces, real agreements so people feel safe, and room to be vulnerable without being tidy about it.

Do 10 to 20 percent of this year and you'll find meaningful progress in your household.

The questions you're already asking

Fair enough

I don't have an hour a week

You do, and you already know where it is. The first experiment is to find it and hold it for yourself. What would you love to say no to, so you can say yes to this?

My partner would never do this

The front door is for the first person in the household brave enough to walk through it. Bring home what you learn and share the invitation. A mandate is just you trying to fix someone else, which I learned the hard way, so don't push.

This sounds a little woo-woo

Part of what makes hard things hard is feeling the hard feelings. We're going to do that here, together, so nobody does it alone. Most of it is experiments, reports, and people keeping each other company while they do hard things.

What if I join and don't use it?

That's what the application is for. If you can't picture yourself showing up, or what's possible for you by being here, maybe this isn't your place.

What if not enough people sign up?

This year needs at least 50 households. If fewer than 50 have joined by October 15, everyone gets a full refund, and I'll share what I learned.

What do I actually get on day one?

A welcome session, a virtual community and a place to introduce yourself, and a calendar of events to join.
