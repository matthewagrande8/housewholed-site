the housewholed experiment

It's not a knowledge problem. It's a practice problem. Hard things are easier in community.

The question

What do I want for my household?

For me it comes down to four things.

  • Myself. That I take care of myself physically, mentally, emotionally, socially.
  • My partner. That I'm a loving and supportive partner to my wife.
  • My daughter. That I'm a present and nurturing dad.
  • Providing. That I provide for my family in a way that doesn't compromise the first three.

This seems clear as day to me. I believe these are the things I want, and I believe a lot of other people want them too.

The rub: I'm not taking the best care of my body. I often feel disconnected, and scared to communicate with my wife. I'm not always present and nurturing with our daughter. And I haven't been consistently providing for my family.

It isn't for lack of wanting these things. It's the things that get in the way. Inaction instead of action. Consuming instead of producing. Hiding instead of putting myself out there. And it's a domino effect: the one thing underneath all four is the same thing. A fear of rejection. A fear of unworthiness.

So this is a place for us to try to be the way we want to be. To take all that knowledge we've consumed and turn it into real action, and support each other in it.

We all need accountability. We all need cheerleaders and uplifters, especially on the down days.

I want to know what your household wants. I want to know what's getting in the way. And I want to know what it could look like a year from now, if you faced those things in loving, supportive community with other people.

The one idea

It's not a knowledge problem

We have every bit of knowledge that ever existed in the world at our fingertips.

It's not that we don't know what to do. It's that we need support and courage to do it.

So much of what's offered to us points outward: the answer is over there, and here is the clear pathway to the thing that will make you whole. This is the opposite of that. It is rooted in experiment, and in company while you run it.

We already have all the wisdom we could ask for. Our problem is practice.

One more thing: yes, this is another screen

I know it is, and I don't want to do this on social media. I really don't. I believe deeper connection and real interaction between humans can happen virtually, in the right container, with the right agreements. My hope is that the work we do here makes us show up differently, more authentically, in our households and in the physical communities we're already part of.

And the honest advantage of a virtual room: it's a little easier to practice these very hard things than it is sitting across from someone. So let's share our wants and our obstacles. Let's feel whatever feelings come with them. Let's design experiments, and then support each other in running them.

The rhythm

A week in the life

You wake up
Excited, or funky, and you say it out loud

Celebrate it, or just name it, on the Housewholed Line. Or post it to the community if today is a day you need support.

Monday morning
The pep talk

You listen to my Monday post. If you were around for the Monday Morning Pep Talks, you'll remember these. Some hope, some encouragement, some wisdom, and an experiment for the week.

When you need to be heard
A listening partnership

You post a request: a space to be heard in whatever is present that day. You lend your ear to someone else. You both walk away seen, heard, and lighter.

When the work is hard
Coworking

You join a session so you're not alone today. You say what you're working on, dig in for an hour, and report back at the end. You keep track of everything you actually got done.

Once a month, or more
A workshop on the thing that's pressing

You're having a hard month with the thing. So are other people here. A community member or a seasoned practitioner walks you through real exercises and experiments, together, and you leave with more clarity and a better relationship to it.

When you need an answer
The Commons

You want a story, a perspective and an experiment. You check the Commons and find one good resource, not a thousand. If it isn't there, you post "tell me your experience with..." and people answer with stories and experiments. That ends up in the Commons too.

End of the week
The celebration post

You close the week and add to your own highlight reel: what went well, what you're proud of, and what still needs attention.

The spaces

What's here for you

  • The Housewholed Line. Call and say it out loud, any hour. Somebody is listening.
  • The weekly pep talk and check-ins. Hope, encouragement, and an experiment for the week.
  • Listening partnerships. Fifteen minutes each, or thirty. One person shares, the other listens. Not to fix, question, advise or judge. Then you return the favor.
  • Coworking and labs. Company while you work, and a room where you share the experiment you ran and what happened.
  • Targeted workshops. Being the under-earning partner. Parenting. Sick and dying parents. Fertility struggles and other pathways to family building. Health and fitness. Troubleshooting medical mysteries together. Healing inherited patterns.
  • Expression sessions. One for each of the emotions, where we experiment with ways of getting it out: writing, singing, dancing, drawing, acting, shouting.
  • The Housewholed Commons. A knowledge base we build ourselves. The things we'd want our younger selves, or our kids, to know and remember.

What you'll bring

  • The money. $1,500 for the household, for the year.
  • The time. At least an hour a week for fifty-two weeks. Hopefully more.
  • Your wants and your challenges, up front and once a month.
  • Saying the hard things out loud.

Where the hour and the money come from

You already make daily investments of time and money that do not serve you, and do not encourage you to do the hard thing.

Take the time and the money from those things, and put them into this.

How would one hour taken from social media, or television, or something else that isn't serving you, benefit you if you spent it working toward something you really want, or really want to overcome?

Across a year, $1,500 is about four dollars a day.

Who this is for

A household is whoever you share a home and a life with. One person, a couple, roommates, a family of any shape. All welcome.

Households where at least one of the four is straining: how you care for yourself, how you are with your partner, how you are with your kids, or how you provide.

Households that feel the way I do. Although we may have some of what we want, there are still things we are yearning for that feel just out of reach.

Households carrying something that feels completely their own, and turns out not to be.

Households who can see the patterns and conditions they did not create, but that are influencing their home.

People willing to own their part. We can all be victims, and we can all own our part in our healing.

Who this is not for

If you want a curriculum, or somebody to hand you the five steps, this is not that, and I would rather you kept your money.

If you are in crisis right now, you need more than a community. Please get real support first, and come find us after. In the US, call or text 988.

And if talking honestly about your household with other people makes you want to close this page... that is worth listening to. This asks something of you.

Why me

I have been convening people my whole life

Twenty years coaching leaders and teams. The last decade helping people relate differently to work, money, family and themselves. Clients from Google, Goldman Sachs and the White House, advising the Vatican and the European Parliament.

And I have been convening people my whole life. Pick-up games as a kid. A two-hundred-person happy hour in South Philly. A coworking crew, a kindergarten family meetup, porch coffee for my neighbors. This is the thing I do. I am finally pointing it at the thing that matters most.

For the record, the rest of it: I left corporate. I lost my father. My wife and I lost three pregnancies before our daughter arrived. A few weeks ago I worked out what our household actually costs to run and we are about eight hundred dollars a month short. Every month.

I do not think that makes me unusual. I think that makes me a household.

What I can and cannot promise

I cannot promise I will get it right. Trying to make sure I get it right is most of what got me into this mess in the first place.

Here is what I can promise. I will create spaces that are caring. There will be real agreements in place so people feel safe. There will be room to be vulnerable and not be tidy about it.

And this, from the version of me that has run this for years in smaller rooms: if you do 10 to 20 percent of this year-long journey, you will find meaningful progress in your household.

The rules of the room

No ads. No trolls. No performance.

Bragging and "this is what is hard right now" are welcome in the same conversation, on the same day, from the same person.

No ranking how one household handled a thing versus another. The value is a new angle, not a scoreboard.

No advice-giving. Share what happened to you. Let people take what is useful.

What's shared here stays here. And as little economic leakage as we can manage: what this community earns mostly stays in it.
