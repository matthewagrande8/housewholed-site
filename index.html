The one idea

It's not a knowledge problem

We have every bit of knowledge that ever existed in the world at our fingertips.

It's not that we don't know what to do. It's that we need support and courage to do it.

We already have all the wisdom we could ask for. Our problem is practice.

The rhythm

What a week actually looks like

Within an hour of waking
Your intention, out loud

I share my intention for the day, and what might get in the way. And I can ask for support in that.

At lunch
A listening partnership

Fifteen minutes each, or thirty. One person shares, the other listens. Not to fix you, not to question you, not to advise you, and not to judge you. Just to see and hear you. Then you return the favor.

Once a week
The live hour

Not ask-me-anything. Share the thing you're working on. Here was my problem, here was my hypothesis, here is the experiment I tried, and here is what happened.

Whenever it hits
The line

Call and say the thing out loud. Maybe there's not somebody on the other line answering, but there will be somebody listening. I'll listen to all of them.

For what's pressing
Workgroups

Parenting toddlers. Sick and dying parents. Connecting more deeply with your partner. Healing patterns. We raise our hands and form teams of support around them.

When it needs to move
Room to move it

Writing, singing, dancing, drawing, acting, shouting. Whatever wants to move.

All year
A commons we build ourselves

Our own body of knowledge, our own household manual, and an inventory of experiments people actually ran, with what happened. Experience, not more information.

What you'll bring

  • The money. $1,500 for the household, for the year.
  • The time. At least an hour a week for fifty-two weeks. Hopefully more.
  • Your wants and your challenges, up front and once a month.
  • Saying the hard things out loud.

Where the hour and the money come from

You already make daily investments of time and money that do not serve you, and do not encourage you to do the hard thing.

Take the time and the money from those things, and put them into this.

How would one hour taken from social media, or television, or something else that isn't serving you, benefit you if you spent it working toward something you really want, or really want to overcome?

Across a year, $1,500 is about four dollars a day.

Who this is for

A household is whoever you share a home and a life with. One person, a couple, roommates, a family of any shape. All welcome.

Households that feel the way I do. Although we may have some of what we want, there are still things we are yearning for that feel just out of reach.

Households who can see the patterns and conditions they did not create, but that are influencing their home.

People willing to own their part. We can all be victims, and we can all own our part in our healing.

Who this is not for

If you want a curriculum, or somebody to hand you the five steps, this is not that, and I would rather you kept your money.

If you are in crisis right now, you need more than a community. Please get real support first, and come find us after. In the US, call or text 988.

And if talking honestly about your household with other people makes you want to close this page... that is worth listening to. This asks something of you.

Why me

I have been convening people my whole life

Twenty years coaching leaders and teams. The last decade helping people relate differently to work, money, family and themselves. Clients from Google, Goldman Sachs and the White House, advising the Vatican and the European Parliament.

And I have been convening people my whole life. Pick-up games as a kid. A two-hundred-person happy hour in South Philly. A coworking crew, a kindergarten family meetup, porch coffee for my neighbors. This is the thing I do. I am finally pointing it at the thing that matters most.

For the record, the rest of it: I left corporate. I lost my father. My wife and I lost three pregnancies before our daughter arrived. A few weeks ago I worked out what our household actually costs to run and we are about eight hundred dollars a month short. Every month.

I do not think that makes me unusual. I think that makes me a household.

What I can and cannot promise

I cannot promise I will get it right. Trying to make sure I get it right is most of what got me into this mess in the first place.

Here is what I can promise. I will create spaces that are caring. There will be real agreements in place so people feel safe. There will be room to be vulnerable and not be tidy about it.

And this, from the version of me that has run this for years in smaller rooms: if you do 10 to 20 percent of this year-long journey, you will find meaningful progress in your household.

The rules of the room

No ads. No trolls. No performance.

Bragging and "this is what is hard right now" are welcome in the same conversation, on the same day, from the same person.

No ranking how one household handled a thing versus another. The value is a new angle, not a scoreboard.

No advice-giving. Share what happened to you. Let people take what is useful.

What's shared here stays here. And as little economic leakage as we can manage: what this community earns mostly stays in it.
